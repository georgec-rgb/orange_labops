01 · The read

The month closed above target, and week 4 was the best week of it

Emerging · week 4

ELISA broke at three labs in one week

HYD fell from 97.21% to 80.00%, BLR 2 reported 82.43% in its first week of reporting the line, and NOA came in at 91%. One week is not a pattern — but three labs at once, on a manual batch platform, in the week the month otherwise peaked, is worth a question rather than a wait.

Shared-system incident

Attune and Superlab went down at three labs on 27 August

BLR 1 lost both for an hour with partial interfacing, HYD for thirty minutes, and NOA saw Attune interrupted for twenty-two minutes — the same day, the same platform, three labs, logged separately. Nobody has connected them.

Clean labs

MUM held above 98% every week

MUM's machine average ran 99.26 / 99.26 / 98.50 / 98.35, August 98.84 — no line below 90% in any week. Its only blemish was a three-hour Vitros fault on 30 August that took the OCD line to 93.98%, its first sub-95 reading of the month.

What the month's shape says. Instrument breakdown never left — five or six labs every single week, which makes it background cost rather than a signal. Two categories retreated to nothing: duplicate barcodes went from two labs to none, and reagent supply and audit each fired once and never again, while facility works stopped after week 2. 05 · Month-end pattern classification

Cleared, persistent, emerging, one-off

With four weeks closed the classification firms up. A cause that ran three weeks and stopped is cleared; one that ran all four is structural; one that appeared only in the final week is a question for September rather than a conclusion.

Structural · all four weeks

GGN power supply

Power was lost in every week of August — roughly 2 hours, then 8, then 19.5, then 4 hours 50 minutes. The pattern is not a trend, it is a constant with a bad week in it. Every GGN measure moves with the outage calendar, and the lab closed the month worst in the network on Consumer OT, Lab OT, accession and machine average alike.

“Power Supply issue on 26th Aug 2026 from 1:50PM to 4:00PM Power Supply issue on 27th Aug 2026 from 8:20AM to 11:00AM” — GGN downtime note, week 4

Structural · all four weeks

GGN Vitros/OCD line

77.41 → 86.13 → 74.26 → 76.24. The only chronic fault in the network that did not break. It fell to 48.61% on 27 August during the outage, but also to 77.29% on 30 August with no outage that day — so power explains the worst days, not the baseline.

Structural · and now the lead item

GGN manpower

First cited in week 3 as one of three issues. By week 4 it is the only item in the lab's issues line, and the first line of all four of its weekly notes. Bulk samples and multi-floor transport sit under it, unchanged since week 2.

“1.Shortage of manpower.” — GGN issues note, week 4, in full

Watch · fell every week

HYD accession OT

98.34 → 98.18 → 97.70 → 96.61. Still on target, and one of only two measures anywhere to decline in all four weeks — the other is MUM's Vitros/OCD line, 97.21 down to 93.98. Alongside it: the lab's machine average finished at its monthly low of 95.48%, downtime doubled to 12 hours, and biochemistry manpower was named twice. Nothing is breaching yet, which is exactly why it needs raising now.

Cleared inside the month

BLR 1 Vitros/OCD — the month's biggest single fix

Three weeks never above 81%, then 91.32%. Breakdown hours at the lab fell from 31.5 to 8 hours 15 minutes, its lowest week of the month. ES Flex and the Morphle scanner dropped out of the notes entirely; the Alinity and D-100 faults that remained ran well under two hours each.

Cleared inside the month

Duplicate barcodes

Cited at both Bengaluru labs in weeks 1 and 2, at GGN in week 3, and at no lab in week 4. BLR 1 accession Part A followed it up: 68.05 → 73.00 → 75.90 → 82.80. Still 15 points below Part B in the same week, so the fix is not finished — but it is moving for the first time.

Cleared inside the month

NRL's slide, and BLR 2's accession

NRL Consumer OT stopped falling and recovered to 94.50%, with one bad day on 24 August on Influenza and H1N1 volume. BLR 2 accession Part B, which had fallen three weeks running to 88.40%, came back to 92.75% once the manpower gap closed.

Emerging · week 4 only

ELISA, and Attune/Superlab

  • ELISA below target at three labs at once — HYD 80.00%, BLR 2 82.43%, NOA 91%. Only one cause is recorded: morning-collected samples arriving already in breach.
  • Attune and Superlab down at three labs on 27 August — logged separately at BLR 1, HYD and NOA, never connected.
  • MUM's first slip — Part B to 95.52% and the OCD line to 93.98% after a three-hour Vitros fault on 30 August.
One-off · closed

Five events that will not repeat

  • GGN NABL audit, 8–9 August — took two consecutive days to 33.43% and 70.46%.
  • BLR 1 distilled-water plant blockage, 10 August — stopped every Alinity module at once.
  • BLR 1 Morphle scanner, 11 August — 26 hours, the longest single outage at that lab all month.
  • BLR 1 lab painting and haematology flooring — admin works inside processing hours, twice, and not since week 2.
  • BLR 2 OCD reagent supply, 5 August — one day at 68.66%, never recurred.
The weekend rhythm held all month. BLR 2 and HYD degrade on Saturday and Sunday — “Weekend high workload” in week 1, and HYD's machine lines dipping on 22 August at 724 orders and again on 29 and 30 August at 679 and 688. Four weeks of the same shape, on the same two labs, with the cause written down each time. It remains the cheapest thing on this page to fix.
06 · Lab notes

Eight labs, in the order they need attention in September

1 · GGN · August Consumer OT 87.17%

The one lab that did not participate in the recovery

Weekly Consumer OT of 82.07, 92.84, 83.86 and 90.12 — its best week still the network's worst. Lab OT closed at 85.70% for the month and the machine average at 90.35%, both last by a wide margin. Power failed in all four weeks. The Vitros/OCD line never cleared 87%. And the lab's own issues line, which in week 3 listed three problems, now lists one: shortage of manpower.

Two details cut against blaming power alone. On 30 August, with no outage, accession Part B fell to 71.74% and the OCD line to 77.29%. And bulk samples with multi-floor transport have been named as a bottleneck in every note since week 2. Power is the biggest cause; it is not the only one.

2 · HYD · August Consumer OT 97.68%

Second-best on the month, and the only lab trending the wrong way

Consumer OT held above 97% every week — but accession OT fell in all four (98.34 → 96.61), the machine average finished at its monthly low, downtime doubled to 12 hours, and ELISA dropped from 97.21% to 80.00% with no cause recorded. Biochemistry manpower was named on 26 and 28 August, and high sample load on 29 and 30 at 679 and 688 orders.

None of it is breaching. All of it is pointing the same way, which is why HYD is second on this list rather than sixth.

3 · BLR 1 · August Consumer OT 95.65%

The month's biggest turnaround, with one gap left open

Consumer OT rose every week to 98.08%, Lab OT gained 7.21 points, the Vitros/OCD line came off three weeks below 81% to 91.32%, and downtime fell to 8 hours 15 minutes — its lowest week of the month. ES Flex and the Morphle scanner dropped out of the notes entirely, and what Alinity and D-100 faults remained were short.

What is left is accession. Overall closed the month at 88.22%, worst in the network after GGN, because Part A ran 68 to 83% against Part B at 95.69%. It is improving fast and barcodes went unmentioned in week 4 — worth confirming that is a fix and not a reporting gap.

4 · BLR 2 · August Consumer OT 95.77%

Recovered fully, then found a new line

Consumer OT rose every week from 89.71% to 98.72%; Lab OT gained 14.43 points. Accession Part B, which fell three weeks running to 88.40%, recovered to 92.75%. The month figures for Lab OT (92.81%) and Part B (92.30%) are carried almost entirely by week 1.

New in week 4: the ELISA line reported for the first time, at 82.43%, with the cause given as morning-collected samples arriving already in breach. That is a scheduling question, not an instrument one.

5 · NRL · August Consumer OT 94.65%

Slide arrested, root cause unchanged

Three weeks down to 92.17%, then back to 94.50%. Lab OT recovered similarly. But the cause named in week 4 is the same one as week 3 — Influenza and H1N1 sample volume, which took 24 August to 89%. Downtime was nil in weeks 1 and 4 and an hour in week 3, apart from the week 2 spike.

Stabilised rather than solved. Seasonal panel capacity is still the open question.

6 · PNQ · August Consumer OT 92.43%

7 · NOA · August Consumer OT 96.97%

8 · MUM · August Consumer OT 97.99%

Cleanest lab of the month

The only marks against it: Part B slipped to 95.52% in week 4, the OCD line to 93.98% after a three-hour Vitros fault on 30 August, and the 90-hour downtime figure in week 1 still has no explanation attached.

Data quality · 1

“Breakdown hours” still means three different things

BLR 1 logs lab-impacting downtime. BLR 2's 105 hours and MUM's 42 are cumulative unavailable hours for a single analyser. NRL reports a wall-clock duration rather than a sum. Unchanged for four weeks. Agree one definition for September.

Data quality · 2

Figures the review cannot interpret

MUM's 90 hours in week 1 and 4 hours in week 3 carry no note. PNQ reported zero for three weeks then left week 4 blank, and its machine lines are empty for the whole month. Otherwise discipline held: every OT reading below 95% and every machine reading below 90% in August carries a note, with one exception — BLR 2's week-4 Part B at 92.75%, which appears only inside the accession note with no cause given.

Data quality · 3

Misplaced measures persist

Machine lines carrying an overall lab TAT figure instead of the machine's own appeared in every week of the month — five instances in week 4 alone. It reads as the wrong measure in the wrong place, and it makes the machine block harder to trust than it should be.

08 · Carrying into September

What August leaves open

Three of the four chronic faults cleared, so the September agenda is shorter than the August one — but it is not the same list with items ticked off. Two of the five items below did not exist four weeks ago.

Agenda · decisions, not updates

Five items that need an owner

  1. GGN, as a whole. Power lost in four of four weeks, the Vitros/OCD line never above 87%, manpower now its only stated issue, and bulk-sample transport unresolved since week 2. This is one decision about one lab's capacity and infrastructure, not four separate agenda lines.
  2. ELISA across HYD, BLR 2 and NOA. Three labs below target in the same week on the same platform, with one cause recorded between them. Establish whether this is batching, staffing or instrument before it becomes September's chronic fault.
  3. HYD's drift. Accession down four weeks running, machine average at its low, downtime doubled, biochemistry manpower named twice. Nothing breaching — act while that is still true.
  4. Attune and Superlab. Down at three labs on 27 August, logged three times as three local incidents. Confirm whether this is a shared dependency and who owns it.
  5. Breakdown-hours definition. Four weeks of a figure that means different things at different labs, plus MUM's unexplained 90 hours and PNQ's blank. Fix the definition before the September numbers are collected, not after.

How to refresh in one pass

  1. Pull each week's figures for Consumer OT, Inhouse Lab OT, accession overall and Part B, the instrument lines and breakdown hours, plus order counts for weighting.
  2. Read every delay note against those measures. Skip Fatal Delays.
  3. Tag each note to the eleven categories in section 04. Add a category only if two or more labs cite something genuinely new — nothing in August required one.
  4. Re-run the classification in section 05 at month end: four weeks makes cleared-versus-structural a real distinction, which one or two weeks cannot.
  5. Close the loop on the watchlist above, then take the agenda in as a month view rather than four week views.
