05 · Week-by-week pattern classification

Escalating, recurring, resolved, one-off

The same cause appearing three weeks running is a system that needs changing. The same cause appearing once is a Tuesday. Sorting the window this way is what makes the monthly review different from three weekly reviews stacked up.

Escalating across all three weeks

GGN power outages

Roughly 2 hours, then 8, then 19.5 — the third week included a single twelve-hour overnight loss. Every GGN measure tracks the outage calendar: Consumer OT, Lab OT, and the OCD, Mindray, Rapid, Biorad and AVE lines.

“Power Supply issue on 22nd Aug 2026 from 12:50PM to 1:25PM and 6:00PM to next day 6:00AM” — GGN weekly note

Escalating across all three weeks

GGN AVE-772 machine OT

95.53 → 91.76 → 82.46. It fell in week 2 on days with no outage, so this is not purely power. Worth an engineer's view before the review rather than after it.

Escalating across all three weeks

NRL Consumer OT and Lab OT

Consumer 97.53 → 94.30 → 92.17; Lab 98.09 → 96.03 → 94.72. No note was filed in week 1, and weeks 2 and 3 name three unrelated causes — a micro approval lag, a Dengue ELISA interface failure, and an Influenza/H1N1 volume spike. That is the profile of a lab running without slack, not a lab with one fault.

Escalating across all three weeks

BLR 2 Part B accession

95.35 → 92.26 → 88.40. One of nine measures that fell every week, and the only one where the lab named the cause both times it commented: manpower shortage, and duplicate barcodes on urine containers.

Recurring in all three weeks

Vitros and OCD machine OT

BLR 1 at 77.37 / 80.23 / 79.67 and GGN at 77.41 / 86.13 / 74.26. A different failure mode each week — well-wash, uWell, versatip, microtip, reflectometer, metering, tip locator — but the same platform. BLR 2 has climbed out of it; HYD sits in the low-to-mid nineties.

Recurring, and migrating

Duplicate barcodes

Cited at both Bengaluru labs in weeks 1 and 2, then at GGN in week 3. Accession Part A at BLR 1 is the visible symptom: 68.05 → 73.00 → 75.90 against Part B at 95%. Rates quoted in the notes ran 60–80% in week 1, then blood at 40–50% and urine at 70–80% in week 2.

Recurring in all three weeks

Alinity module and reagent carousel

Present every week at BLR 1 and in the first two at BLR 2, with a different trigger each time: module stop, loader queue error, distilled-water plant blockage, power interruption, then three separate reagent-carousel failures in week 3. Same subsystem, rising frequency.

Recurring across sites

AVE interface and result transfer

Results failing to transfer from AVE analysers appears at HYD and BLR 2 in week 2, and at BLR 1 in week 3 — the last running a full eight hours. HYD also lost result transfer from a Mindray analyser in week 1. Cross-site and vendor-level rather than local.

Resolved inside the window

Four causes fixed and held

  • BLR 2 Alinity — 88.26 → 94.65 → 97.92 after the 7 August module stop.
  • BLR 2 OCD — 73.37 → 88.63 → 91.32 once the reagent supply cleared.
  • BLR 1 AVE 773 — 77.87 → 99.55 → 97.80 after the departmental manpower warning.
  • PNQ Consumer OT — 87.50 → 90.54 → 93.45, three straight weeks of gain.
One-off, no follow-up needed

Events that will not repeat

  • GGN NABL audit, 8–9 August — took two consecutive days to 33.43% and 70.46%.
  • BLR 1 distilled-water plant blockage, 10 August — stopped every Alinity module at once.
  • BLR 1 Morphle scanner, 11 August — 26 hours, the longest single outage at that lab in the window.
  • BLR 1 lab painting and haematology flooring — admin works landing inside processing hours, twice.
One weekly rhythm worth naming. BLR 2 and HYD both degrade on Saturday and Sunday — BLR 2's week 1 notes read “Weekend high workload” for both days, and HYD's OCD and Alinity lines dip on the same two dates. HYD repeated it on 22 August at 724 orders. That is a rostering pattern rather than an instrument pattern, and it is the cheapest thing on this page to fix.
06 · Lab notes

Eight labs, in the order they need attention

1 · GGN · Consumer OT 82.07 / 92.84 / 83.86

A power problem wearing an operations problem's clothes

Three weeks, three different headline causes — NABL audit, then outages, then outages plus manpower — but the outages are the through-line and they are growing. The lab's machine-OT average fell to 86.79% in week 3, the worst in the network. Underneath sit two structural constraints the team has now put in writing twice: bulk sample batches, and sample transport across multiple floors at peak hours.

“As we evaluated mostly orders out of time ~ 5 minutes only.We are looking for interns to solve samples transportation challenges from accessioning to the floors at peak hours.” — GGN accession note, week 2 “1. Shortage of manpower. 2. With current manpower its very difficult to run two batches of Dengue 3. request to process both OCD all parameters daily for evening” — GGN issues note, week 3

The second is a formal escalation sitting in a spreadsheet note. It should be an agenda item with an owner.

2 · NRL · Consumer OT 97.53 / 94.30 / 92.17

Sliding quietly, three weeks running

The only lab with no single villain and a consistent downward slope on both Consumer and Lab OT. Week 2 was micro reports held for next-day doctor approval; week 3 was an Influenza/H1N1 spike on 22–23 August plus an ELISA fault on 21 August that pushed report saves past 1 am. Downtime also spiked in week 2, largely a nine-hour Dengue ELISA interface failure.

Only one of the three named causes is panel volume; the others are a process delay and an interface fault. If the slope continues into week 4, the question is seasonal capacity as much as instrument reliability.

3 · BLR 1 · Consumer OT 94.26 / 95.02 / 95.40

Improving on the surface, two chronic faults underneath

Consumer OT climbed all three weeks and Lab OT held above 91% after week 1 — but this lab carries the two most persistent misses in the window. Vitros/OCD machine OT never cleared 81%. And accession Part A sat between 68 and 76% against Part B at 95%, which localises the loss entirely to the receiving step: duplicate barcodes running 40 to 80% depending on specimen type.

Downtime peaked in week 2 on two genuine one-offs — the Morphle scanner at 26 hours and ES Flex at eleven and a half — plus admin renovation switching instruments off mid-processing.

4 · BLR 2 · Consumer OT 89.71 / 97.19 / 98.43

Best recovery in the network, with one measure still falling

Consumer OT gained 8.72pp and Lab OT 14.65pp off a bad first week — the Alinity module stop on 7 August alone took that day's Lab OT to 57.14%. Machine OT is now the second-cleanest in the network.

The exception is accession Part B: 95.35 → 92.26 → 88.40, falling every week, with manpower shortage named in week 2 (three people on leave) and again in week 3. This is the one place where a recovering lab is still losing ground.

5 · PNQ · Consumer OT 87.50 / 90.54 / 93.45

Improving fast, but the cause is a handover, not a lab

Lab OT is at or near 100% and accession OT is 100% across all three weeks — the lab itself is clean. Every named cause is a shift handover: samples transferred late, or approved at eight in the morning. Machine OT is unreported, so nothing contradicts that reading, but nothing confirms it either.

6 · HYD · Consumer OT 97.34 / 97.95 / 97.99

Absorbing real downtime without breaching

Held above 97% every week while carrying meaningful instrument loss, including a six-and-a-half-hour OCD metering failure on 10 August and a four-hour AVE transfer failure on 15 August. The only visible cracks are weekend and high-load days: 22 August at 724 orders took Alinity to 87.28%, and a biochemistry manpower gap on 20 August took it to 79.15%.

7 · NOA · Consumer OT 96.47 / 98.50 / 95.37

Solid, with a week 3 instrument cluster

The week 3 dip is fully explained by three overlapping breakdowns on consecutive days — the Morphle scanner, Mindray, and AVE-772, the last of which alone took its own day to 24.76%. AVE-772 has now failed on a microscopic-capture fault in two of the three weeks; that is the item to watch.

8 · MUM · Consumer OT 97.33 / 98.01 / 98.45

Cleanest lab in the window

Improving every week on Consumer OT, machine OT averaging above 98% with no instrument below 95%, and no OT-related commentary required. The only open item is a reporting one — see below.

Data quality · 1

“Breakdown hours” means three different things

BLR 1 logs lab-impacting downtime. BLR 2's 105 hours and MUM's 42 are cumulative unavailable hours for a single analyser. NRL reports a wall-clock duration rather than a sum. Agree one definition before week 4 is reviewed.

Data quality · 2

Two unexplained figures

MUM's 90 hours in week 1 and 4 hours in week 3 carry no explanatory note. The 90 is the second-largest single figure in the window and the review cannot interpret it. Otherwise discipline is good: every OT reading below 95% and every machine reading below 90% in the three weeks carries a note.

Data quality · 3

Duplicate and misplaced notes

Two BLR 1 measures carry near-identical notes filed twice — one pair by two different people, one pair by the same person. And at least a dozen machine lines carry an overall lab TAT figure instead of the machine's own, which reads as the wrong measure in the wrong place.

08 · Monthly review pack

Carrying this into the review when week 4 closes

The three weeks here plus the week to 30 August are August. The structure below is what that fourth week slots into.

Agenda · decisions, not updates

Five items that need an owner

  1. GGN power resilience. Outage hours roughly doubled twice over, including a twelve-hour overnight loss. The question for the room: backup cover for the analyser bank, or accept GGN OT below target through the season?
  2. Vitros and OCD platform review at BLR 1 and GGN. Three weeks below 81% and 87% respectively, with a different subsystem failing each week. A vendor and preventive-maintenance conversation, not a weekly firefight.
  3. Duplicate barcodes. Urine specimens running 70 to 80% at the Bengaluru labs and now appearing at GGN. It caps BLR 1 accession Part A in the low-to-mid seventies and no lab-side action will move it.
  4. NRL capacity for the Dengue and Influenza season. OT has fallen every week and the third week's cause was panel volume.
  5. Accession manpower at BLR 2. Part B down every week to 88.40%, with manpower named twice.
Watchlist · check these first in week 4

Ten readings that decide whether the trend holds

  • GGN Consumer OT and Lab OT — recovery, or a third breach?
  • GGN breakdown hours — anything above 20 confirms escalation.
  • NRL Consumer OT and Lab OT — a fourth consecutive fall makes it the month's headline.
  • Vitros/OCD machine OT at BLR 1 and GGN — above 85% would be the first break in the pattern.
  • BLR 2 accession Part B — below 88.40 means four straight weeks down.
  • BLR 1 accession overall, and the Part A figure behind it.
  • GGN AVE-772 — three weeks declining already.
Method · reproduce this for week 4

How to refresh in one pass

  1. Pull the week-4 figures for Consumer OT, Inhouse Lab OT, accession overall and Part B, the instrument lines and breakdown hours, plus order counts for weighting.
  2. Read every week-4 delay note against those measures. Skip Fatal Delays.
  3. Tag each note to the eleven categories in section 04. Add a category only if two or more labs cite something genuinely new.
  4. Re-run the four-way classification in section 05: a cause present in all four weeks moves from recurring to structural; a cause present only in week 4 is a one-off until the next week says otherwise.
  5. Close the loop on the watchlist above, then take the agenda in as a month view rather than four week views.
Scope, stated

What is and is not in this analysis

  • In: Consumer OT, Inhouse Lab OT, accession OT overall and Part B, machine processing OT, and breakdown hours — for all eight labs.
  • Excluded on instruction: Fatal Delays per 1000. Twenty-seven notes filed against it in these three weeks were dropped before any tagging or counting.
  • Read but not analysed here: CP approval, report automation, indent bounce rate, SKU fill rate and inventory gap. Available on request.
  • Source: 134 delay notes across the three weeks once Fatal Delays was removed, of which 102 sit against the measures in scope above.
