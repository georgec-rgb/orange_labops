Consumer OT impact

How transfer delays translate into Out-of-Time orders

4.3% of tests (502) went Out of Time against Ideal CT overall — and 99.6% of them are directly attributable to transfer delay: they would have met Ideal CT if transfer had taken the row's logistic TAT. Transfer eats 65–70% of the 16 h Ideal CT budget, while actual lab-side time after the department scan is under an hour. The single biggest driver is the 3–8pm collection window: those samples miss the daily van, and with a 16 h Ideal CT they go OOT 73–90% of the time despite being only ~5% of volume. Week-wise, OOT went 1.4% → 11.7% (bad week of 6 Jul, when even 6–7am samples had 16–17 h transfers) → 5.0% → 0.9% → 5.1% → 5.7%. One caution: the stellar 20 Jul week is partly test mix — 59% of that week's tests carried a 29 h Ideal CT; like-for-like on 16 h-CT tests it was 2.5%, still the best. At order level, 12.6–16.3% of orders went OOT in the last two weeks, up from 2% in the best week.
