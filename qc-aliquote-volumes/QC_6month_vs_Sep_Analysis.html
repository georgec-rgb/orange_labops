1. The achievement

Following the QC aliquot-volume optimisation (reduced control volume pipetted per run, effective ~30-Aug), September QC material cost fell at five of six labs versus their pre-change run-rate — a net saving of ₹1,44,360 per month (−10%), roughly ₹17.3 lakh annualised. The one lab up is MUM, and that is new specialty-test scope, not waste.

3. What drove it — QC aliquot-volume optimisation

The volume of control material pipetted per QC run was cut on the high-use controls, so each vial yields many more runs and fewer vials are opened per month. Lead examples:

  • Assayed Chemistry Control L1 (C-31): ~300 µL → 20 µL (≈93% less material per run).
  • Assayed Chemistry Control L2 (C-31): ~300 µL → 50 µL (≈83% less).
  • Further immunoassay / specialty controls trimmed or consolidated across the ~45-product QC list.

4. NOA — why the Mar–Jun base

NOA's QC reporting had a gap in July & August — the Stago (coagulation) and Biorad controls (~₹6.5k/month) were not captured, so August was understated. Add them back and August ≈ ₹1.29L, in line with September. September is NOA's first fully-reported month. Measured against NOA's clean Mar–Jun run-rate, NOA is down 19% — one of the strongest reductions. Action: fix NOA's QC reporting so Stago/Biorad controls are captured every month.

5. MUM — the one genuine increase (+₹12,319)

MUM rose because of new Alinity specialty-immunoassay testing, which brought new QC controls (Alinity QC +₹12,033; OCD +₹6,518; AVE & Alifax actually fell with the aliquot change). The specific items that increased:

6. Bottom line

  • ₹1.44 lakh/month saved (−10%) from the aliquot optimisation — 5 of 6 labs down, led by BLR2 (−21%) and NOA (−19%).
  • BLR1 still cut 7% while adding new machines.
  • MUM is the only rise, and it is new specialty-test QC scope, not a leak.
  • NOA reporting to be fixed so monthly figures stay clean.
