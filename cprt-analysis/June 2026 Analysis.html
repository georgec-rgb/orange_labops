NOA — A mix problem, not a cost problem

On the true January baseline, NOA’s cost per test rose +6.5% (₹49.5 → ₹52.7) while volume grew only +2.9% — consumption outran volume. But this is mix, not cost: reagent cost per test actually eased slightly (rate ≈ −₹0.0, favourable); the whole rise came from the basket shifting toward costly immunoassays. The counterfactual proves it — had June kept January’s test mix, cost/test would have been ₹49.47, below January’s own ₹49.49. Drivers: Vitamin B12 +₹48k, Insulin +₹32k, hs-CRP +₹29k, Lipase +₹16k grew, while cheap high-volume routine (CBC/ESR −₹19k, Urine −₹11k) declined. It remains the lowest-GM lab (~67%), but the lever is test-mix, not consumption control.

2 · Cost per test, volume & CPRT — four months

Cost per test & volume by month (network)

CPRT (after OCD & ex-validation)

Cost per test and CPRT move together across the four months: a dip through the April conversion, a spike in May, and a return to the March level by June. Volume was ~760k (Mar), ~648k (Apr), ~657k (May) and ~776k (Jun).

In plain terms

Nothing structurally worsened between March and June. Splitting the change by cause, each lab's own margin is flat and the shift of business between labs is slightly favourable. Reagent consumed per test is actually a touch lower in June than March across the network — i.e. no efficiency loss. In numbers, this is a ₹6.7L saving: had June run at March's reagent efficiency it would have cost ₹6.7 lakh more — so consumption per test improved (led by HYD ₹4.9L and BLR 1 ₹2.7L). The only real wobble was May's one-off validation booking, which reversed.

Yes — June is back to the March baseline. Vitamin D is the clearest case: ₹72.3 (Mar) → ₹67.9 (Apr, under-costed in conversion) → ₹81.7 (May, correction overshoot) → ₹72.4 (Jun) — back to March. Most Abbott parameters land within ~₹1 of their March cost. Overall network cost per test is ₹50.5 (Mar) → ₹49.9 (Jun) — a shade below March, i.e. fully normalised, marginally better.

This confirms the mechanism: April's high margin was artificial (Abbott parameters booked below CPT during the platform conversion), May's dip was the correction (plus the ₹12.3L validation booking), and June is the true run-rate — which equals March. The only parameters running slightly above March are TFT (+₹0.5) and B12 (+₹1.4) — minor, worth a light watch.

Bottom line

  • There is no real margin decline. From March, GM is flat (~73.5%).
  • No over-consumption problem — consumption per test is flat-to-lower vs March.
  • May's dip was a ₹12.3L validation timing entry, now reversed.
  • Cost growth is volume, seasonality (Dengue) and revenue-backed mix — a pricing/mix watch, not a waste problem.
  • Monitor: BLR 1 Alinity QC (up ₹0.8L vs Mar), GGN revenue-per-test, NRL/NOA structural margins.
